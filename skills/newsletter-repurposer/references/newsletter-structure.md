# Newsletter structure, the body component and the shell

Read this with `references/brand-brain/examples.md` open. That file holds the creator's real
newsletters and it is the target. This file names the patterns that make a post turn into a good
email. Where the real newsletters and this file disagree on style, the real newsletters win.

The short example lines below are illustrative, made up to show a shape. They are not anyone's real
copy and must never be reused.

---

## The boundary that matters

A newsletter is made of two things:

1. **The post derived body.** The part that comes from the Instagram post. The creator's points, in
   their voice, in newsletter shape. This is a **component**. It is written so it can be lifted whole
   and dropped into a different newsletter shell without a single edit.
2. **The shell.** Everything wrapped around the body: greeting, offer mention, reply prompt, sign off.
   This skill writes one shell, the flat newsletter.

Many creators later move to a sectioned newsletter, where the post derived body becomes one section
inside a longer email. **So never write the body and the shell as one blended piece of prose.** Build
the body first, on its own, then wrap it. The body must not reference the greeting, the sign off, or
anything else in the shell. Get that boundary right and a second format is a template swap. Blend them
and it is a rewrite.

---

## The body component

Written from the source post and nothing else. Three beats, in order:

**1. The way in.** One to three short paragraphs. Either a personal moment from the post, something
the creator noticed ("I don't know if you've been noticing the same thing…"), or the reader's own
thought said out loud ("I hear this ALL the time"). It comes from the source. If the source has no
story in it, open on the problem the post solves. Never invent a memory.

**2. The turn.** The line where the story becomes the point. One or two lines, never a paragraph.
"And that is when I realised something nobody talks about."

**3. The teaching.** The actual points from the post. Two shapes, pick by content:

- **Listed**, when the post has distinct points. Each item is a marker, then a short label line, then
  one to three sentences underneath. Use the list markers from `references/brand-brain/voice.md`, one
  marker for the whole list, never mixed in one email.
- **Flowing**, when the post is one idea explored. Short paragraphs, one thought each, no list.

Every substantive point in the source appears here. Nothing else does.

The body ends on a line that lands the idea. It does not hand off to a CTA, that belongs to the shell.

## The flat shell

```
[greeting]

[BODY COMPONENT]

[soft offer mention]

[reply prompt]

[sign off]
```

**Greeting.** The creator's own, from `voice.md` or the real newsletters. Use the merge tag exactly as
written in `examples.md` so the email platform picks it up. It can also fold into a first line ("I
know, {first name}…") if the real newsletters do that.

**Soft offer mention.** One offer, from `references/brand-brain/offers.md`, mentioned as the obvious
next thing, never a pitch. Shape: "And if you want something that makes this feel less overwhelming,
my [offer] breaks it down step by step."

**Reply prompt.** Specific, one word or a short phrase to reply with, the way the real newsletters do
it ('Reply "YES" if you want the next part'). Optional. Use it if most real newsletters have one.

**Sign off.** The creator's own sign off and name, exactly as in `voice.md`.

## Liftable sections

Whoever edits the draft has to be able to delete or reorder a block without unpicking the rest:

- One blank line between blocks. Never run two blocks into one paragraph.
- No cross references. Never write "like I said above", "the second point", "as we saw", or number the
  points in prose. If a list item is deleted, nothing else should now read as broken.
- Each list item stands alone and carries its own label.
- The offer mention and the reply prompt are separate blocks, not one sentence, so either can be
  dropped.

## Subject lines

Three options every time, that differ in angle, not three rewordings of one. Match them to the real
subject lines in `references/brand-brain/examples.md`: their length, their casing, their punctuation,
their emoji habit.

Patterns that usually work for creator newsletters, used only where the real ones support them:

- **Short.** Two to six words.
- **Curiosity first.** The email is the payoff. Never a summary of the content.
- **Lowercase leaning**, if that is how the creator writes.
- **A trailing ellipsis**, a confession in parentheses, or a bare number or flat statement.
- **One emoji at most**, never at the front.

---

## Mechanics

Take every mechanic from Register B (email) in `references/brand-brain/voice.md`: exclamation density,
CAPS habits, ellipses, emoji placement, where the humour lives, "&" versus "and". Always:

- Second person, one reader, never "you guys"
- Contractions and short sentences
- No dashes as punctuation, ever
