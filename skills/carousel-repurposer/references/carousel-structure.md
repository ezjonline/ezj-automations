# Carousel structure, from a reel script to slides

Read this with `references/brand-brain/examples.md` open. That file is a carousel the creator actually
posted, next to the reel script it came from, and it is the target. This file names the patterns that
make the translation work. Where the benchmark and this file disagree on style, the benchmark wins.

**The deliverable is text.** Slide number, slide title, slide body. The creator builds every visual
themselves. Nothing in this file describes a layout, a template, a font, a colour or an image, and
nothing you write may either.

The worked examples below are illustrative, made up to show the mechanics. They are not anyone's real
content and must never be reused as copy.

---

## Slide 1 is the hook, rebuilt

A reel script usually carries a hook block: a visual hook, a talking hook, and sometimes a text on
screen hook. **Use what is there. Never invent a new one.**

The talking hook is written to be heard once, at speed. Slide 1 is read, in silence, by someone
deciding whether to swipe. So the job is a translation, not a transplant.

| | |
|---|---|
| Talking hook in the script | "I film a whole week of content in two hours, I'll show you exactly how" |
| Slide 1 title | Film a week of content in one sitting |
| Slide 1 body | SO YOU STOP SETTING UP YOUR CAMERA EVERY SINGLE DAY |

The promise survives. The first person setup and the "I'll show you" do not, because the carousel
itself is the showing. The body carries the payoff, the reason to keep swiping.

**The rule.** Title carries the promise in the fewest words that still say what it is. Body carries
the payoff. If the script has a text on screen hook, it is usually closer to slide 1 than the talking
hook is, because it was already written to be read. Prefer it.

If the hook block is empty, build slide 1 from the script's own opening line. That is still the
creator's material. It is not an invitation to write a new hook.

**Attribute the result, never promise it to the reader.** If the hook says "it saved me ten hours a
week", that is a thing that happened to the creator. Writing "it saves you ten hours a week" turns
their result into a guarantee the script never made. Keep the number, keep the person: "It saved me
ten hours a week." Or swap the number for a benefit line. Either is honest. Promising it is not.

## What becomes a slide

**One distinct thing per slide.** When the content is a process, each slide is one step, with the step
number in the title and the instruction as the title itself:

```
1. Write every hook before you film
2. Change your top between every video
3. Film all the talking parts first
```

Number the titles when the content is genuinely sequential. Do not number them when it is a list of
qualities, reasons or observations, because numbering implies an order the script does not have.

**The body is one to three plain sentences.** It says why the step matters, what happens if you skip
it, or the detail that makes it work. It does not repeat the title.

**Keep the spoken emphasis.** If the script says "this is the important one!!", the slide says it too.
When the script leans on a point, the slide leans on it.

**Not every line in a script becomes a slide.** Apply this test, in order, to every line:

1. **Can the reader do something with it, or know something they did not know?** If yes, it is
   substance and a candidate for a slide.
2. **If no, it is narration.** Lines describing what a tool is doing while you wait, how something
   felt, what the camera is doing, or what happens off screen are narration.
3. **Narration never gets its own numbered step title.** Numbering a narration line tells the reader
   it is a step they have to perform, and it is not.
4. **Fold it, or drop it.** Fold narration into the neighbouring slide's body only when it changes how
   the reader acts, for example a warning to wait rather than intervene. Otherwise leave it in the
   reel and say so in the notes.

This test is also what keeps slide counts stable between runs on the same script. If two runs disagree
on the count, it is almost always a narration line one run promoted and the other folded.

**A dense point may overflow into a second slide.** Label it "[continued]" in the title. Continue the
point rather than compressing it or dropping half of it.

**More than one reel may feed one carousel.** If two scripts are pasted, treat them as one pool of
points and say so in the notes.

## How many slides

**Count the distinct points the script makes, drop the narration, add slide 1 and the CTA slide.**
That is the number.

Sanity range: roughly five to ten. Below five and a carousel is usually a single image. Above ten and
you are asking for a lot of swipes. If the arithmetic lands outside that range, the split is probably
wrong, so look again before forcing it.

**Say the number and the reason in one line**, in the notes block, never on a slide. For example:
"Seven slides: the hook, the five steps the script walks through, and the CTA. The line about waiting
for the export is narration, so it sits in slide 5's body."

## The goal shapes the writing, and only the writing

The goal takes one of three values: `saves`, `sales`, `followers`.

**The goal never changes which points are covered.** Every substantive point appears in all three
versions. What changes is emphasis, the framing of the hook, how much of the body is instruction
versus meaning, and the last slide.

| Goal | What the reader should do | Slide 1 leans | Bodies lean | CTA slide |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `saves` | keep this to come back to | the completeness of the thing, "everything you need to…" | specific, dense, exact settings and numbers, the parts worth screenshotting | asks for the save first, then mentions the offer |
| `sales` | want the thing you sell | the result the script contains, not the completeness of the process | compressed, argued, proof promoted, see the lever below | the offer is the ask |
| `followers` | follow you | the hot take or the identity, "this is how I…" | shorter, punchier, more opinion and less procedure | asks for the follow first, then mentions the offer |

**Which offer the CTA names is not decided here.** That is rule 2 of `references/output-rules.md`, and
topical fit decides it. The goal decides only how the ask is worded.

Nothing here selects a style, a template or a design. If a run produces anything about how the slides
should look, it has gone wrong.

### The `sales` lever, in full

`saves` adds detail and `followers` cuts it, so each already changes the shape. Left as a wording
instruction, `sales` changes almost nothing, and two runs on the same script come out near identical.
So `sales` changes **structure and order**, not adjectives.

- **Lead with the result.** Slide 1 names the outcome or the number the script contains, not the
  completeness of the process. Only what the script contains, attributed to the creator, never
  promised to the reader.
- **Compress the procedure.** Merge adjacent mechanical steps into one slide. A sales carousel is an
  argument, not a reference card, so the body must come out **measurably shorter** than the `saves`
  version of the same script.
- **Promote proof.** Where the script contains a concrete result, a number, a named brand, or a before
  and after, promote it into a title or give it its own slide, positioned before the CTA. Only from
  the script.
- **Give the CTA runway.** The second to last slide names the gap the offer closes, using only material
  from the script and the Brand Brain. The last slide makes the ask.
- **Same points, different shape.** Coverage stays at 100 percent. What changes is emphasis, order, and
  how many slides carry them.

**The check.** Against the `saves` version of the same script: at most one identical slide, plus at
least one real structural difference, meaning a different slide count, a different order, or a body at
least 15 percent shorter. If a `sales` run cannot show that, rewrite it before handing it over.

## The CTA slide

The last slide is the payoff line plus one CTA. A common shape:

```
And that is how you film a week of content in one sitting!

COMMENT BATCH and I'll send you my filming checklist
```

A comment to DM trigger, the word in caps, and the thing they get named plainly. The trigger word is
usually tied to the content. Use the trigger words listed in `references/brand-brain/offers.md` or the
script's own.

**The CTA comes from `references/brand-brain/offers.md` and nowhere else.** Never name a price, a bonus,
an inclusion or a product that is not in that file. Which one to name is decided by rule 2 of
`references/output-rules.md`. When nothing fits, use the freebie and say so in the notes.

If the script already ends in a CTA of the creator's own, and it maps to a confirmed offer, use theirs.
If it points at something listed as unconfirmed, do not name it, and flag the gap in the notes.

## Register on the page

This is the Instagram register (Register C in `references/brand-brain/voice.md`), usually drier than a
caption, because the decoration lives in the design, which the creator handles and you do not touch.

So on a slide, unless `voice.md` or the benchmark says otherwise:

- Second person, direct, contractions
- Short sentences. One idea each
- The creator's own emphasis where the script has it: "!!", CAPS on a whole phrase, "&" if they use it
- Parenthetical asides carry the aside, never the headline
- No emoji in slide copy unless the script or the benchmark carries them
- No dashes as punctuation, ever. See `references/output-rules.md`
- No markdown. No bold markers, no headers, no bullet characters the creator would not type

You are writing to one person holding a phone. Not to an audience, and not in the voice of a brand.
